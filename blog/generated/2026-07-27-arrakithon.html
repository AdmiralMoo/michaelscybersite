The Arrakithon
https://www.michaelscybersite.net/blog/2026-07-27

Fans of classic rock are all familiar with the concept of a Zeppathon: to listen to Led Zeppelin’s entire discography of studio albums in order in one sitting. It’s a fantastic experience and I would highly recommend it—I just had my own Zeppathon last month, in fact.

There are plenty of different kinds of —thons you can have with other bands. A Supertramp marathon is a Superthon, for example. “—thon” doesn’t work with every band, though: what would you call a Genesis marathon? A Genocide? Not all names are created equal.

Anyhow, I’ve recently found myself doing a Dune marathon of sorts. It was only meant to last one day, but it’s expanded into something much greater.

It all started out when I found myself wanting to re-read Herbert’s original 1965 classic which kickstarted the franchise. I looked down at my Blu-Ray shelf and realized I hadn’t yet watched Dune: Part Two*, though I’ve owned it for a couple years.

Why not rewatch the movies and then reread the book? Sounds like a good time.

I’ve been a fan of Dune (1984) for a long time, and I saw Dune: Part One when it came out in theatres. Unfortunately, I never had a chance to see Part Two* in theatres, but that didn’t detract from how good it was when I finally got around to watching it.

Part One was as good as I remember it being. There was a collective murmur in the theatres when the title card popped up on screen; I don’t think there was any mention of there being a part anything when it was first being promoted. It was a really well done movie that’s hard to criticize because of its fantastic acting, production and visual effects.

Part Two was just as good, really. It lived up to everything I’d hoped it would be. Both films showed many more scenes than David Lynch’s original adaptation did—splitting the book into two halves was the right choice.

I wish they showed the dinner scene from the first half of the book, and I don’t particularly like how Chani is so anti-everything in Part Two. It really makes me curious as to how Messiah will be adapted into Dune: Part Three.

It makes sense for a third movie to be dedicated to Messiah since that book feels more like an appendix or an epilogue to the original Dune; you could’ve easily just tacked it on as the book’s fourth part. I really hope the adaptations continue after that—I wouldn’t mind it if Villeneuve continued milking this franchise since there’s so much fantastic material I’d love to see on the screen.

After watching the first two Villeneuve movies, I rewatched Lynch’s Dune for what seems like the tenth time. It’s wackier and more esoteric-feeling than the new adaptations. I like the casting better in Lynch’s version in pretty much all departments. Although Zimmer’s soundtrack feels very alien and fits the new movies well, I’ll always love Toto’s soundtrack.

If the original Dune movie didn’t exist, it wouldn’t be a problem that there is no Patrick Stewart or Sting in Dune: Part One or Part Two. But Dune (1984)* does exist, and the lack of those two actors feels like a genuine absence. I read the book before I saw the movie, but now that I’m re-reading it, the face of Gurney Halleck is unavoidably Patrick Stewart.

It’s all fantastic, really, and I find I don’t prefer one over the other. They each have their strengths and they each have things I wish were slightly different. I hope that the Villeneuve adaptations continue to shine bright and become for Sci-Fi what Peter Jackson’s The Lord of The Rings is for Fantasy. Lynch’s version will always be wacky and fun to watch no matter what.

That brings us to the books.

The last time I read the first book was before I suffered a massive head injury nine years ago. I’ve forgotten a lot of things from the time before so some things feel like I’m experiencing them for the first time. Dune was no different, though some of it felt odd.

Some parts of the book were a shock because it felt like the first time I’d read it, but then I would remember my reaction as it was many years ago. Sometimes, I would remember what I’d visualized in my head before I’d seen the movie. It was quite an odd and fascinating experience.

Needless to say, I flew through the first book, and I just finished rereading Messiah today. I couldn’t wait for tomorrow to begin reading Children of Dune—I just got through the first couple pages this evening to whet my appetite.

I’ve had a bit of a Dune marathon going for the past week. Considering it’s going to go on for quite some time now that I’ve committed to the books, you could call it an Arrakithon.

The “—thon” moniker works well for what I believe is the greatest Sci-Fi franchise of all time.

I’ve decided that I’m going to read Brian Herbert’s Sandworms of Dune and Hunters of Dune, which I believe were the final books based on his father’s outlines for the original series’ conclusion before his death. I’ve only ever read the Frank Herbert books, so it’ll be interesting to get some of those questions answered and see—hopefully—a more definitive ending.

I’m having a fantastic time immersing myself in this top-quality science fiction once more. A Zeppathon is something you can do many times over, but how many people can withstand multiple Arrakithons?

This is a massive commitment: to watch all three of the movies currently released, and then to read the first eight books in the Dune franchise? Insanity! Fun! It’s the summer of Dune and I’m just having the time of my life.
